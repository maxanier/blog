Depending on where you are traveling in Latin America, you may need hard cash instead of a credit card.
However, sometimes it can be difficult or expensive to withdraw money at an ATM.

## Argentina 2026
This is probably the most problematic one due to inflation and money politics.
Additionally, you will need cash unless you are only visiting very touristy places such as Buenos Aires or Patagonia.
If you can, bring USD notes and exchange them at a trusty exchange. Compare exchange rates.

**ATM fees:** With an international credit card, all ATMs I have seen charge between \$10 to \$20 regardless of the amount.
Additionally, you often cannot get larger amounts of cash, while at the same time your home bank may have a minimum for international withdrawals.

**WesternUnion:** With WesternUnion, you can transfer money to your self, i.e., send money from your home bank account/credit card and pick up the transferred amount at a local WesternUnion branch in Argentina.
We did this once per person, because a) as a new customer you get one-time off the (proportional) fees and b) WU had a better exchange rate than my credit card.
But you need to register online at WesternUnion with all your personal (passport details), send the money, find a well visited WU branch in and then potentially wait in line to pick up your cash at the counter.
However, I have heard that it is possible that the WU branch does not have enough cash. The smaller the branch, the more likely.
In one situation, this process got me 264k pesos at WU instead of 250k pesos at an ATM for 165€, so around 6\%. Depends on your situtation if this is worth the trouble.


## Chile 2026

**ATM fees:** The Banco del Estado de Chile charges the lowest fees, although I only checked very few alternatives.

## Peru 2026
In Peru, you can often get both Peruvian Sols and USD.

**ATM fees:** The Banco de la Nacion does not charge any fees! But it only has Peruvian Sols.
You can only withdraw up to 400 Sol in one transaction, but you can just repeat the process.

For USD, you seem to always pay around \$10, but you can get larger sums.
I have seen \$400.
